combat

*-Space Wolves: Howlin' Stars-*
A bounty hunter/merc who is a newcomer to the Orion system, puts together a
peculiar crew, The Space Wolves, and visits colonial hubs for odd jobs
(capturing criminals, tracking down escaped prisoners, missing persons,
satellites or equipment as well as kidnapping VIPs, robbing/smuggling
equipment/shipments/tech, etc.)
These are times of war and the use for a bounty hunter/merc is deeply
appreciated, hence the player can choose to favor one faction over the other
and subtly decide the outcome of the conflict.
Working steadily for a faction rewards with exclusive and powerful equipment
for ground and/or space combat, but also means losing favor with
previously-neutral/friendly factions.
Several mercs compete for some of the Wolves' targets as well, providing
emergent foes who either appear during or after completion of contracts to
reclaim the proof of a finished task (and hence the prize).

There would be no fast travel, as in, the player must flight/drive his/her
ship/truck to the destinations/points of origin, thus providing the
opportunity for the random encounters to occur ("space roadblocks" by
opposite factions, space pirates, alien raiders, aliens/terrans in distress,
space station/ship/random planet help signals, local fauna, etc.)

The ship, called *Howlin' Star* (just "the Howler" to the crew), is at first
a space junk, and it is given as compensation for one of the first contracts
completed by the team. It is customizable tho, allowing for weapondry and
defensive system upgrades, as well as the look of it (not the hull, but the
paintjobs).

The truck is dubbed *Blood Claw* (just "the Claw" to the crew), and it's
initially a decommisioned septic tanker found in a scrapyard during escape
in one of the missions. As with the ship, it is customizable in certain ways
(paintjobs, weapondry, defensive systems). It is only used in certain ground
missions.

The *Space Wolves* are a highly specialized crew, with each member working a
specific job on and off the Howlin' Star/Blood Claw:

   - There's a cook who also works as overseer during ground missions, used
   as comic relief by providing really bad intel on targets and random ranting
   on the com. For example, in one of the missions the team would steal a cargo
   whose contents are supposed to be advanced alien weapondry, only to find it
   full of dirty laundry.
   - There's a tech specialist, focused on bypassing security, hacking
   robots and repairing the ship/truck. His/her tech expertise level would
   affect which defensive systems are able to be installed on the ship/truck.
   - There's a weapons specialist, focused on heavy weapondry and setting up
   perimeters of auto-turrets for missions that require it, as well as
   maintenance of the guns on the Howler/Claw. His/her weapon expertise level
   would affect which weapondry is able to be installed on the ship/truck.
   - There's a medical specialist, focused on the well being on the group by
   assisting the ground incursions and using his/her psychology degree to keep
   the crew from derailing. His/her psychology expertise level would affect the
   loyalty of the crew.

Consequences of low "crew loyalty":

   - the desertion of a low-loyalty crew member.
   - if the "feeling" is across all members, the player suffers a crew
   mutiny and is stranded in some planet, along with the cook. It would also
   mean the player encounters the previous crew in certain missions and must
   fend them off, with the ultimate stand-off taking place in one of the final
   faction missions (with the ex-team mates banding with an opposite faction).
   Emergent mercs on common missions would be replaced by the previous team on
   a counted number of ocassions (for dialogue sake).

Either case would require the player to recruit new talent/get a new ship.

The player: a man/woman with an unknown past, some say an ex-con, some
others say ex-military or war criminal, but what all agree upon is that
there are years and years of experience behind him/her, with an earned
reputation on the system that's respectable enough for the potential
crew-members to be interested in following him/her (recruitment missions for
each of the crew members would be applicable: "i'll scratch your back if you
scratch mine first").

Each completed contract would reward the player with either credits,
equipment or both. Credits could be spend on equipment, or courses for the
crew-membes to improve their skills.
Equipment is comprised of: armor sets and personal/ship/truck
weapondry/defensive systems, as well as personal weapon add-ons.
Such items can also be either traded with space scavengers or sellers, sold
or destroyed to be used as scrap. Scrap is used for ship/truck repairs. The
efficient use of scrap is dependent on the tech skill of the tech
specialist.

the game would end with end of the war (with the most favored faction
winning the conflict), and thus, with no other contracts to take upon, the
player could either retire (non-canon) or part to another galaxy (sequel,
kekekaka)

the game would not take itself too seriously, with some outrageous names
appearing on towns, planets, enemies and the like, as well as crew members
promptly joking about it and making humorous remarks at certain situations
or enemies faced, tho some themes would be properly dark (slavery, drug
trafficing, experimentation, etc.). Aimed rating: Mature.

*-Space Wolves: Warmongers-*
After leaving the Orion system, The Space Wolves arrive at the Anayhala
galaxy, hungry for new contracts and credits, but they're taken by surprise
when this new place has no war going on. Instead, it is a peaceful union of
various factions, dubbed The Anayian Confederacy, who have had "the longest
of peace times ever recorded in history", so it's up to the crew to stir
things up and start a galactic conflict by sabotaging, kidnapping, framing
and stealing that will actually put them on the run from the law.
After being captured by the Anayian SpecOps and put in the maximum security
prison on the system, the crew must escape, reclaim their ship and somehow
frame their getaway to a few of the Anayian factions, hence shattering The
Great Anayian Pact and effectively starting a war.

The faction mechanism previously used in the first game would re-appear
here, with contracts from all the factions of the previous Anayian
Confederacy available, except for those which were framed by the Wolves of
their escape, who in turn become the main antagonists (basically the player
would be choosing their own worst enemy).

What could a group of four do against a galactic-sized force?
